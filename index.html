⚠ Rules of engagement

Practice offensive techniques only against systems you own or are explicitly authorized to test — the platforms above, your own VirtualBox lab, or a written-scope engagement. Scanning or attacking anything else is illegal under laws like the U.S. CFAA, regardless of intent. This maps directly to Canon I and Canon II of the Code of Ethics.

The whole ladder, not just the next rung

Certification map

Where every major credential sits, who it's for, and what it demands. Your current target — ISC2 CC — is flagged. Each tier is labelled by the level of the professional it's built for.

How to read this

Certs don't replace hands-on skill — they package it. The strongest path stacks a credential with proof: CC or Security+ plus documented lab work (your write-ups, a GitHub of solved boxes) makes you far more hireable than the paper alone. Experience-gated certs like CISSP, CISM, and OSCP come after you've done the work; treat them as a horizon, not a next step.

ISC2 Code of Ethics · a condition of certification

The four canons

Every ISC2 credential — CC through CISSP — requires you to commit to this code. The canons are listed in priority order: when two conflict, the higher number wins. Society before self, integrity before client.

Preamble The safety and welfare of society and the common good, duty to our principals, and duty to each other, require that we adhere — and be seen to adhere — to the highest ethical standards of behavior.

Commit to the code

This unlocks the offensive trainers in The Range. It mirrors the real ISC2 application: you attest to the canons before you carry the credential.

How it's enforced

Members who knowingly violate any canon face a peer-review panel that can revoke certification. Standing to complain follows the canons: anyone may report a Canon I or II breach, only principals (employers/clients) may report Canon III, and only other members may report Canon IV. Observing a violation and staying silent is itself a Canon IV breach.
